🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Modern appraisal goes beyond keyword volume to evaluate phonetic fluency and structural patterns (like CVCV).
  • Length is a primary multiplier: 4 to 6-letter .com domains carry a massive premium in the brandable market.
  • Our algorithm cross-references recent, verified aftermarket sales (comps) to ground valuations in current market reality.
  • We provide both Wholesale (investor-to-investor) and Retail (investor-to-end-user) price ranges for complete clarity.

1. Linguistic & Phonetic Scoring

The foundation of our algorithm is linguistic analysis. An invented name like Vofira has zero historical search volume, but our system recognizes its high "phonetic transparency." It evaluates the name against the Radio Test: Can it be spelled correctly after being heard once?

The algorithm also identifies valuable structural patterns. Names following the CVCV (Consonant-Vowel-Consonant-Vowel) pattern, or those ending in high-value tech/bio suffixes (like -os, -ia, or -ex), receive a significant scoring boost because historical data shows these structures sell faster and at higher multiples to end-users.

2. The Length Multiplier

In the brandable domain market, length is king. Our algorithm applies a strict length multiplier. A 4-letter pronounceable .com is exponentially more valuable than a 9-letter one. The sweet spot for modern startup branding is 5 to 7 letters. Our system heavily weights domains in this range, as they are visually balanced, easy to type, and fit perfectly on a mobile app icon.

3. Comparable Sales (Comps) Analysis

An appraisal is only as good as its grounding in reality. The AutoDNG engine continuously ingests data from verified public domain sales (via NameBio and other aftermarket databases).

If you appraise Bytezeo.com, the algorithm doesn't just guess. It looks for recent sales of similar 7-letter, tech-focused, invented .com domains. If similar names have recently sold in the $2,500 to $4,500 range, our algorithm anchors its valuation to that real-world data, adjusting for current market trends and inflation.

4. Wholesale vs. Retail Valuation

One of the biggest points of confusion in domain investing is the difference between wholesale and retail pricing. Our tool explicitly provides both:

  • Wholesale Value: This is the "quick sale" price. It represents what a professional domain investor would pay you today in cash to add the domain to their portfolio. This is typically 15% to 30% of the retail value.
  • Retail (End-User) Value: This is the target price when selling directly to a funded startup or enterprise that specifically wants that exact name for their brand. This is where the 4-to-5-figure payouts happen, but it requires patience and outbound marketing.

By providing both metrics, we empower you to make informed decisions. If you need quick liquidity, you know the wholesale floor. If you are holding for the perfect buyer, you know the retail ceiling to aim for.
